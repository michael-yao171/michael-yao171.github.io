Quantifying Athletic Neck Guard Comfort and Restriction

In progressExpected April 2027, IBEHS 3I06 Injury Biomechanics, Dr. Cheryl Quenneville’s lab Designing a mechanical test rig and human testing protocol to measure interface pressure, flexibility, and overall comfort of cut-resistant hockey neck guards. Read the case study
Role
Mechanical Design and Testing Lead
Type
Course project and research
Tools
SolidWorks

Problem

Mandatory hockey neck guards often face poor player compliance because of stiffness, heat, and restricted neck movement.

Current standards test cut resistance but lack quantitative benchmarks for comfort and mobility.

Approach and progress

Conducting a study with hockey youth participants to analyze the range of motion and perceived comfort of multiple commercially available neck guards.

Design a testing rig based on ASTM D4032 to measure the flexibility of the neck guards to provide a quantifiable “comfort” level.

Next steps

Finalize the materials and methods documentation for McMaster Research Ethics Board (MREB) approval.

Recruit hockey player volunteers to establish baseline subjective comfort scores and compare them against the test rig data.

Hook, Hip, & Sinker: Osseointegrated Hip Implant Design

2023, IBEHS 1P10 Health Solutions Design Project, presented at the Faculty Poster Showcase Designed a patient-specific total hip replacement featuring a spiked, porous stem geometry and developed a Python computational model to evaluate fatigue life under osteoporotic bone conditions. Read the case study
Role
Computing Sub-Team Lead and Project Coordinator
Team size
4
Tools
Python, Autodesk Inventor, FDM 3D printing

Problem

The patient was a 59-year-old woman with concurrent osteoarthritis and severe osteoporosis, experiencing joint pain and fatigue.

The implant had to endure dynamic loads for 10 to 15 years without mechanical failure.

Design process

Selected porous Ti-6Al-4V alloy and 3D woven composites (3DWCs) for the stem to lower stiffness and reduce stress shielding, paired with a Zirconia Toughened Alumina (ZTA) acetabular liner for fracture toughness and wear resistance.

Developed the main driver function and the fatigue analysis subprogram to model cyclical loading, evaluate stem diameters, and quantify cyclic stress fatigue limits across material options.

Testing and results

Validated stem fatigue life computationally in Python across variable stem diameters and material properties (Ti-6Al-4V: modulus 31.5 GPa, UTS 305 MPa; 3DWCs: modulus 2.49 GPa, UTS 110 MPa).

Produced a validated 3-part physical prototype and delivered a technical poster presentation and a 10-minute design defense.

What I would change

Modify the downward spike geometry into retractable or tapered barbed profiles to reduce surgical insertion trauma while maintaining pullout resistance.

Real-World Effectiveness of Influenza Vaccination Against Severe Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

2024 to 2025, Miller Lab, Michael G. DeGroote Institute for Infectious Disease Research and McMaster Immunology Research Centre, peer-reviewed publication Conducted screening and data extraction for a global systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating the real-world clinical effectiveness of influenza vaccines in preventing severe hospital outcomes across pediatric and adult cohorts. Read the case study
Role
Co-author and research reviewer
Team
11 co-authors across McMaster, SickKids, and Nazarbayev University
Tools
Covidence
Publication
DOI, PMID 41093140

Research question

How well do influenza vaccines prevent severe hospital outcomes in real-world use, across pediatric and adult populations?

Methods

Used Covidence to run a blinded, dual-reviewer workflow across titles, abstracts, and full texts.

Independently screened over 750 candidate articles against strict inclusion criteria: test-negative designs, laboratory-confirmed hospitalized cases, and defined clinical endpoints.

Extracted study demographics, adjusted and crude odds ratios, and vaccine effectiveness estimates, and assessed risk of bias.

Results

Screening across PubMed, Ovid, and Cochrane CENTRAL took an initial pool of 7,727 publications to 461 full-text assessments and 165 included studies.

Protective effectiveness was up to twofold higher in pediatric patients than in adults, stronger against A(H1N1) than A(H3N2), and higher when seasonal formulations were well matched.

What I would change

Automate the initial title and abstract deduplication and coarse exclusion passes with verified machine-learning screening classifiers in Covidence, to speed up screening.
